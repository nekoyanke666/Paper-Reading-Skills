# Paper Methods

Input: methodological/design question, original material, evidence, and expert profile. Output: intelligible principles, assumptions, and inference limits, or a discriminating design labeled as a new proposal, in the session language.

## 1. Match question and research type

Follow paper-teach and paper-evidence. Identify explanation of an existing method versus follow-up design; restore relevant sources, figures, and expertise and stay within the task rather than starting full guidance.

Empirical work needs design, intervention, measurement, analysis, and inference; theory assumptions, proof validity, and applicability; computation tasks, models, data, comparisons, and reproducibility; qualitative/humanities work materials, analysis, and alternatives. Experimental/statistical items apply only where relevant.

## 2. Explain the existing method

Connect the question, intervention/reasoning steps, measurement/analysis, and obtainable conclusion. Explain the method choice, direct measurement versus proxy, and required premises.

For experiments/statistics, check the source definitions of objects, experimental units, controls, replicates, n, error bars, and tests. Explain effect size, variation, and uncertainty when they matter. Do not assume n means independent individuals or replace interpretation with significance.

For equations explain symbols, units, variable relationships, assumptions, and important derivations. Use small numerical examples where helpful, clearly separating teaching assumptions from the paper's settings. For proofs connect premises to the proposition and identify what remains unproved. Explain procedural purpose and location without inventing author practice.

## 3. Propose follow-up research

Start from a specific unknown and distinguish established results from new hypotheses. Describe the main comparison, intervention/reasoning strategy, measurements/materials, key controls, and how possible outcomes affect the competing explanations. Theory may use counterexamples, weaker assumptions, or propositions; computation datasets, benchmarks, or ablations; adapt other fields accordingly.

Stay at the requested design level. Unreported conditions are not completed or validated author work. Concrete procedures require appropriate source support and the requested scope.

## 4. Return basis and limits

Give source locations and return important claims, conditions, limitations, and unresolved checks for paper-memory. Completion requires an understandable account of how evidence is produced and what it supports, or how a proposal distinguishes hypotheses.
