# Paper Teach

Input: saved settings and expert profile, current topic, shared terms, and prior explanation. Output: an accessible explanation and any terminology/comprehension updates.

## 1. Organize around the reader's goals

Help a reader outside the field understand why the question arose, how methods or arguments address it, which conclusions the evidence supports, and what remains unknown.

Use the confirmed interaction language and plain, accurate wording while retaining the details necessary to understand the research. English skill instructions do not prescribe English conversation. A requested separate interaction language overrides the shared target-language default only when recorded explicitly.

Explain one complete small topic at a time. Avoid front-loading all details or dropping critical assumptions for simplicity. The caller and paper-memory manage the whole outline and segmentation; this interface organizes the current scope.

## 2. Explain terms without replacing the real concept

When introducing a technical term, give its target-language form, original/English term where relevant, and its meaning or role in this study. Do not explain it with more unexplained jargon. Reuse glossary definitions; distinguish homonyms and return terminology corrections to shared memory.

Analogies are allowed when their correspondences and limits are explicit. Then return to the actual disciplinary concept, argument, and method. Do not later treat the analogy as the research object itself.

## 3. Make connections and transitions explicit

When a new topic depends on a previous concept, briefly recall the necessary part and explain the connection. Avoid mechanical repetition, and do not equate “already explained” with “understood.”

Explain why another concept must come first, why the topic is changing, or why earlier material is being revisited. Recall according to the current prerequisite, not a fixed schedule of repeated glossaries.

## 4. Respond to difficulty

If the reader is confused or the pace is too fast, identify the missing premise, reduce the conceptual step, and use a paper-specific example, experiment, or bounded teaching example before returning to the question. Merely paraphrasing the same answer is insufficient. Do not require a quiz to continue unless requested.

Before returning, ensure the necessary terms, premises, relationships, and limits have been explained. Return new terms and the reader's actual questions to paper-memory without assuming comprehension.
