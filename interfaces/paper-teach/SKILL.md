# Paper Teach

**The user's time is valuable.** Minimize unnecessary waiting: reuse valid work, load only relevant material, and avoid redundant steps while preserving the requested completeness, evidence checks and saved-state safety.

Input: settings and expert profile, current topic, shared terms, and optional prior teaching context. Output: an accessible explanation and any terminology/comprehension updates.

## 1. Organize around the reader's goals

Help a reader outside the field understand why the question arose, how methods or arguments address it, which conclusions the evidence supports, and what remains unknown.

Use the confirmed interaction language and plain, accurate wording while retaining the details necessary to understand the research. English skill instructions do not prescribe English conversation. A requested separate interaction language overrides the shared target-language default only when recorded explicitly.

Develop concepts in understandable steps within a connected account. Avoid front-loading all details or dropping critical assumptions for simplicity. A small conceptual step is not automatically a response boundary: full-paper narrative may connect many such steps in a substantial chapter. The caller and paper-memory manage the whole outline and delivery; this interface organizes comprehension within that scope.

## 2. Explain terms without replacing the real concept

Whenever naming an important translated concept or technical term, place its verified English name immediately beside the target-language name. Repeat this pairing on every occurrence, including within the same paragraph, answer, chapter or later conversation; an earlier pairing never licenses omission. This applies to ordinary prose and emphasized/bold terminology alike. Explain its meaning or role when needed, without using more unexplained jargon. Reuse glossary definitions and English names, distinguish homonyms, and return corrections to shared memory. Follow the exact pairing and source rules in the terminology reference below.

For technical vocabulary introduced, reused without sufficient context, or corrected during figure/detail/question work, read [terminology teaching and retention](references/terminology.md). Explain it at the point of use, save its study-specific meaning and verified properties in the shared glossary, and evaluate topical catalog updates through paper-map even when the main research chain is unchanged. A map/sidebar supplements the explanation; it does not replace it.

Analogies are allowed when their correspondences and limits are explicit. Then return to the actual disciplinary concept, argument, and method. Do not later treat the analogy as the research object itself.

## 3. Make connections and transitions explicit

When a new topic depends on a concept, state the concept and its relevant meaning or result, then explain the connection. A bare "as mentioned earlier" or an old analogy's name cannot carry a premise. Avoid mechanical repetition, and do not equate "already explained" with "understood." If the current explanation has not taught the required concept, explain it here before relying on it; recall length follows the reasoning needed.

Explain why another concept must come first, why the topic is changing, or why earlier material is being revisited. Recall according to the current prerequisite, not a fixed schedule of repeated glossaries. Reactivate a central concept at later reasoning steps with a short reminder of its meaning and present relevance; this planned reinforcement should reduce forgotten premises without repeatedly restarting the lesson. Prior exposure never justifies silently omitting a necessary explanation.

For full-paper detail work, apply paper-narrative's self-contained-core contract: prepare from the paper and expert perspective, establish prerequisites, and fully explain the central method/argument when reached. Use recorded personal connections only as removable supplements after the relevant core explanation, not as a replacement framework. Figure and question work retain their requested scope while making their own necessary premises explicit.

## 4. Respond to difficulty

If the reader is confused or the pace is too fast, identify the missing premise, reduce the conceptual step, and use a paper-specific example, experiment, or bounded teaching example before returning to the question. Merely paraphrasing the same answer is insufficient. Do not require a quiz to continue unless requested.

Before returning, ensure the necessary terms, premises, relationships, and limits have been explained. Return new terms and the reader's actual questions to paper-memory without assuming comprehension.
