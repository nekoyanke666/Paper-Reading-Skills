# Complete, Standalone Node Explanations

Read whenever creating, reviewing or revising node.detail, a node's shared glossary definition, or the content shown in its sidebar. This applies to main trees, focused trees, all three detail modes and unconnected catalogs. Concise labels and low-detail membership do not authorize an incomplete explanation of a visible node.

## Explain the actual referent

Write as the saved domain expert for a reader outside that field who opens this node directly from search, without reading a chat, following a route or inspecting a figure first. Start by saying what the node denotes in concrete terms. Then explain the properties, operation or reasoning needed to understand that referent and its role in the current study. Define essential terms locally in the sidebar; links to other nodes offer depth rather than supply an otherwise missing premise.

The node name is concise. Its detail is a complete account of the node's declared scope, not a short label paraphrase, a clipped passage from a lecture, an illustration, or commentary about what the reader should avoid believing. Completeness is judged by intelligibility and substance, not a fixed word count. Do not turn every small node into an unrelated textbook chapter. If one node combines independently meaningful subjects, refine its scope and use additional substantive nodes through the normal identity rules.

Use [Markdown and mathematical text](rich-text.md) to organize the sidebar with paragraphs, emphasis, headings, lists, tables or formulas where they help. Define the mathematical objects and operations in prose as required; a typeset formula is not its explanation. Keep the original Markdown/TeX string in node.detail and related fields, with verified English names beside every important translated term.

Adapt the account to the referent rather than printing a generic form:

- A concept or named object needs a clear definition, relevant structure/properties or distinctions, and its study-specific role. Name the actual objects or alternatives rather than relying on an unspecified "signal," "type" or "state."
- A mechanism needs its participants, the actual process and causal or conditional steps supported by the sources. Explain how the resulting change arises; do not infer a mechanism from an association.
- A method needs its purpose, actual inputs, substantive operations, outputs and interpretation. Explain an essential equation or quantity with its notation and assumptions when the referent requires it; a tool name or an example invocation is insufficient.
- A finding or argument needs the actual observation/comparison or proposition, how the evidence/reasoning supports it, and the scope of the conclusion. Merely naming the figure or saying a claim is supported does not explain the result.
- A question or limitation needs the concrete unknown or boundary, why it matters for this model or inference, and what evidence or missing condition determines it. An unanchored warning is not the subject's explanation.

## Ground the explanation in knowledge, not conversation

Compose the account from current sources, the verified model and canonical glossary meaning. Reuse factual knowledge after checking its fit; do not paste answer fragments or old metaphors because their wording resembles the node name. A saved explanation elsewhere is not this sidebar's content.

Do not write "use the earlier example," "as explained before," "see the figure discussion" or an equivalent unresolved pointer in place of the required account. Explain the relevant entities and relationship here. Do not invent earlier discussion when no record exists.

The default node explanation uses direct disciplinary concepts and the actual study, not invented examples or analogies. Do not use an example as its definition or mechanism. If a requested worked example or a genuinely necessary calculation is added, first provide the complete substantive explanation, explicitly identify the example's role and whether its values are invented, and keep the example supplementary. Removing it must not remove the definition, operation or conclusion.

## Describe findings; do not substitute figure warnings

Figure and Methods locators identify evidence. They are not a substitute for explaining what the node is or what happened. Describe the relevant research objects, measurement/comparison and supported interpretation directly when they belong to this node. A reader should understand the account before opening its source links.

Do not populate a node with statements such as "the object in Figure X is not Y" or "do not treat this panel as Z" when the node is meant to explain a concept, object, method or finding. Such figure-policing comments do not teach that subject. Replace them with the actual definition, process or result. A request to interpret a specific figure belongs to figure/question teaching and still requires a substantive account of what is shown.

Preserve scientifically important assumptions, uncertainty and limits after establishing the subject. State the actual model or measurement condition, its implication and the reason for the inference boundary. This is substantive scope information, not a generic disclaimer. A correction should give the accurate account and its basis; an explicit comparison may state a meaningful distinction. Neither is permission to fill the primary explanation with unrelated "not X" warnings or remove important evidence limits.

## Keep canonical meaning and saved work consistent

The glossary is the shared definition authority. Use that meaning to compose node.detail's complete explanation and keep studyRole, verified properties and source references consistent. Do not maintain conflicting definitions or require the reader to reconstruct meaning from scattered fragments. The existing sidebar may organize the explanation, shared definitions and properties into sections; all necessary substance must be present there. Keep original/English terminology where useful, the confirmed interaction language, and traceable source locations. Distinguish background, findings, hypotheses, teaching illustrations and unverified user notes.

Use the same node ID/content across trees and detail modes. Repair an inadequate same-referent explanation in place, retaining identity, aliases and positions. In managed saved records, reconcile through the existing update workflow; do not overwrite independent user edits or imply that updating a reusable skill has already rewritten old maps. Do not promote user-note drafts into verified scientific content merely to satisfy this contract.

## Expert acceptance review

Before accepting the text, read it as if this node were the reader's first entry into the map:

1. Can the reader say what the referent is, how its relevant process/operation/reasoning works, and why it matters here?
2. Are necessary terms, entities, conditions and comparison objects actually explained in the sidebar, with the technical depth appropriate to this node's scope?
3. After removing old-chat references, invented examples and figure-policing remarks, does a complete positive account remain?
4. Are the description, shared definition, actual sources and evidence limits consistent, without invented facts or irrelevant cautions?

If one answer fails, revise the substance rather than adding more citations, a longer warning, a link to another tree, or a field claiming completeness. These are expert content checks, not quality that a nonempty string or a keyword-matching test can prove.
