# Node Identity and Lifecycle

## Identity versus content

A node represents an identifiable concept, condition, operation, observation, claim, or question. Split two claims that can independently hold or be refuted.

Store id, identity, label, title/detail, type, aliases, provenance, conditions, limits, and status separately. Identity includes kind, scope, and referent, with stable source anchors or claim IDs when needed. Names are not identities: different measurements, objects, assumptions, or scopes do not merge because names match; renaming, translation, English terms, or additional evidence do not create a new object.

## Add

Search current nodes/aliases, glossary, claims, and sources first. Reuse the ID for the same identity. Add a necessary prerequisite, important question/method/observation/claim, critical limitation, connecting concept, or reusable technical term needed to read the paper. A term need not be a new finding to belong in a topic catalog, but incidental mention, paraphrase, or a repeated definition question does not create a new referent.

New nodes need understandable content, source nature, and an appropriate view. Narrative members need a meaningful connected reading path; defined catalog terms can remain unconnected without invented edges or category roots. Keep incidental details not needing lookup/display in full memory or the evidence index. Any substantive node can extend or root a narrative tree; catalogs have a topic and no root. There is no fixed root type or depth. Reuse IDs across views.

## Update

Update the same referent's definition, sources, limits, status, or name in place, retaining ID and adding the old name to aliases. Materially different objects/conditions require another node. Supporting a hypothesis changes its state, not its identity. A classification change needs an explicit unchanged-referent explanation.

Corrections propagate to claims, edges, sources, explanations, and translations relying on the old statement. Retain refuted claims with clear corrections/status/limits; move them to a limitations or alternative-explanation view when appropriate rather than letting them continue to support the conclusion as facts.

Preserve positions unless explicit layout repair is needed for overlap. Language changes preserve identity and aliases; do not duplicate the graph by language.

## Three removal intents

1. **Remove from one view:** change its membership and displayed incident edges only; retain global identity, other views, and history. Replacing a removed root is explicit.
2. **Merge a duplicate:** supply same-referent evidence, keep target ID, merge aliases/sources, reconnect edges/memberships, and redirect old IDs. Matching names alone are insufficient.
3. **Retire an erroneous/out-of-scope artifact:** explicitly handle roots and incident edges, then preserve original content, reason, and revision without erasing sources or research history.

Refutation is not a deletion reason. No silent cascades, damage to shared nodes in other views, or dangling links. Restore with a reason and original traceable identity; do not resurrect merged IDs as separate duplicates.

## Edge lifecycle

Edge identity is canonical source, target, predicate ID, and structured qualifiers. More sources, synonymous labels, localization, or stronger support do not create a new edge. Same key combines sources; different conditions or directed feedback remain distinct. Conflicting statuses require expert review, not silent selection of the strongest claim.

Changed endpoints, meaning, or conditions retire the old edge and add a new one. Explanation, provenance, status, basis, or primary-view flag may update in place. Retain retirement reason/revision and support justified restoration.

## Semantic completeness and shape changes

Before adding a node, apply the [content model](content-model.md): distinguish necessary objects/concepts from repeated phrasing. A comparison question must identify its alternatives; related definitions or objects may extend any node. Choose one of four visual roles from the referent, not a new shape per detailed type. A corrected shape classification keeps ID, aliases, sources, and positions, records its reason, and refreshes shape-dependent routes. New meaning follows the ordinary new-identity rules.
