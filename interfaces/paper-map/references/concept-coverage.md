# Whole-Paper Concept and Relationship Coverage

Read when building a main tree, reviewing its completeness, integrating a full-paper explanation, or responding to a request for a richer map. Connectivity and a tidy diagram do not establish coverage of a paper's concepts.

## Build the concept inventory from sources

Use the verified whole-paper model, actual substantive sections, claims/evidence, and methods to inventory the conceptual content. Track the problem and research objects, required background, prior approaches and their limits, proposed representations/mechanisms/arguments, assumptions, method stages and their inputs/outputs, measurements, datasets/materials, comparisons/controls, major findings, negative results/tradeoffs, and inference boundaries where they matter. Adapt to the discipline and paper rather than inventing an item for every category.

Save a source-to-map crosswalk under progress.map.conceptCoverage. Each item identifies sourceId/location, the concept/claim or relationship, its importance, representation (main, focused, catalog, sidebar, or missing), nodeIds/linkIds/viewIds, and a reason for compact or deferred treatment. Keep status provisional when required source/teaching scope is incomplete. Mark reviewed-full-scope only after actual source review and gap resolution; counts or fields cannot prove semantic completeness.

## Main tree as a complete overview

The main tree must visibly represent the entire research architecture: principal questions, important model/method contributions, evaluation/argument structure, major results, and interpretation limits. Each central contribution or result needs a recognizable substantive node or shared branch anchor in the main view. Do not hide an important new object, method, finding, or tradeoff solely in a long sidebar or assume a generic "results" node covers them all.

Focused trees expand meaningful branches through the exact same global IDs. The main view should make it apparent where the paper's important topics fit and how to reach their detail. It need not display every minor procedure, synonym, equation symbol, or link at once; term catalogs carry lookup detail, and focused views carry dense mechanisms/algorithms/evidence. A node for an algorithm can anchor its internal variables and steps in a focused view while retaining the algorithm's input/output and purpose in the overview.

The eighteen-node threshold triggers a density review, not a cap or a target. Do not delete important concepts or make the overview incomplete just to stay below it. Use spacing, canvas expansion, substantive shared anchors, and focused views while preserving the overview's breadth. The four-relation-type limit remains a readability rule: move a distinct relationship-rich subproblem into a focused view without erasing its connection to the main architecture.

## Meaningful branches and richer links

One-to-many, many-to-one, multi-step branches, and feedback cycles are supported. Represent one-to-many as separate canonical edges from one node to each target, each with its own predicate, evidence state, source, conditions, and explanation. Do not invent intermediary nodes merely to make it look binary. A genuinely joint/simultaneous condition needs its own explicit concept/qualifier; separate arrows alone do not assert a conjunction or prove sufficiency.

For each relationship, state the concrete endpoints and what the connection means in this study. Review whether the source actually supports composition, classification, prerequisite, method dependence, measurement, testing, empirical support, causality, formal implication, or a limitation. Avoid turning the entire map into generic "requires understanding" links when a more precise registered meaning is justified. Conversely, lack of evidence is not permission to add a causal or logical edge for visual richness.

Keep standardized short labels; put the paper-specific meaning, basis, direction, conditions, and caveats in hover/detail content. New evidence may add a qualified parallel edge or a cross-link; the all-relations toggle exposes nonessential cross-links within the current view. Critical refutation or limits must remain visible in the initial view. Do not increase line counts for their own sake, connect every pair, or add vocabulary membership as scientific edges.

## Review and report actual coverage

Compare the crosswalk against the paper, not only against the existing graph. For each substantive contribution/result/method, verify its concepts and key relationships are represented at an appropriate level and reachable from the main architecture where important. Check that terminology definitions exist, branch captions are recognizable, detailed views use shared IDs, and the path from method/argument to evidence and conclusion remains intelligible.

A partial map can be useful during reading, but describe its actual scope and continue filling significant omissions as figure/detail/question work progresses. Do not call it a complete conceptual representation because all nodes are connected or all figure numbers occur. Correct omission, weak abstraction, and ambiguous relations through the normal reviewed lifecycle. Record deferred/nonessential items with reasons rather than asserting an unsupported numerical percentage of understanding or coverage.
