# Topic Orientation, Motivation and Reading Pace

Read for detailed or concise paper explanations. Apply this in chat delivery and saved explanation artifacts. The reader should know what problem is being addressed, why the next topic matters and where it belongs in the paper before the technical account begins.

## Make small topics visible

Use informative Markdown subheadings for small topics within a chapter. Name the actual question, obstacle, decision or result, not an unexplained technical noun or generic "more details." Divide a long passage at a change of question, mechanism, method stage or evidential purpose. A heading does not imply a new response, an obligatory pause or a quiz.

In detail mode, aim for a few short explanatory paragraphs per small topic; roughly two to four is a useful starting point, not a cap on necessary reasoning. Split a longer derivation or multi-stage procedure into named steps while retaining its assumptions and important operations. A segmented chat installment usually handles one coherent research step through about two to four small topics. Prefer a natural smaller chapter over a long block containing an entire source section. An explicit single-delivery request still gets the requested whole, organized with subheadings and short paragraphs.

In simple mode, use the same structure more compactly: normally three to five brief stages for the main story, each with one short paragraph and a second only for an essential prerequisite or inference. Do not import detail mode's exhaustive procedures, every panel or full derivations. Adjust to the actual study and request rather than filling a fixed heading count.

## Orient before explaining

At the start of every small topic, integrate these elements into a short opening, usually one to three sentences; simple mode normally compresses them into one sentence:

1. **Current task:** the specific question, obstacle or inference we are addressing now.
2. **Reason and stakes:** what is unresolved from the preceding result, why this topic can resolve it, and what the reader could not explain or judge without it.
3. **Paper anchor:** the actual relevant text, Methods subsection, figure/panel or supporting-source locator, plus why that material is being introduced here.

These are ingredients of connected prose, not three repetitive administrative labels before every paragraph. A transition such as "Next we discuss the algorithm because it is important" fails: it states neither the unresolved issue nor what the algorithm must accomplish. A figure number alone locates evidence without motivating the topic. For prerequisite background outside the paper, state which upcoming paper claim/method/figure requires it and identify the background source separately. Do not invent source positions to complete the pattern.

A fictional illustration: "The two classes share observed features, so forcing ambiguous cases into the positive group can create false calls. We now examine how the decision rule handles those cases, because otherwise the later gain in precision would be an unexplained number (the fictional Methods decision rule and Results comparison)." Then teach the actual rule, inputs and consequences; the lead is groundwork, not the complete explanation.

## Make the reason-to-result chain explicit

Develop the unresolved problem, its consequence, the requirement it places on a solution, the authors' design/argument, the evidence and what that evidence changes. Name the real objects and alternatives. Explain why the chosen operation/control/comparison can answer the current question, not merely what it does. After the result, state which part of the question it resolves and what remains to motivate the next topic. Recall the specific earlier idea when needed rather than saying only "as mentioned before."

Storytelling means a visible chain of reasons and consequences, not invented discovery chronology or stronger scientific causality. Distinguish a researcher's design rationale from evidence of a causal mechanism. If the source supports association, prediction, an interpretation or a hypothesis, retain that status. Label an expert's reconstructed motivation as reconstruction where it differs from the authors' stated account; narrative smoothness never supplies missing evidence.

## Label topics based mainly on supporting sources

When a small topic is mainly based on Supplementary Information, Supplementary Methods/Notes, Supplementary figures/tables, an appendix or Extended Data, put both the source kind/identifier and reading necessity in parentheses in its heading. In the saved interaction language, use forms corresponding to `(Supplementary Methods; required)` or `(Extended Data; optional)`, with the actual source locator in the lead or heading. Keep publisher-specific identifiers recognizable. Translate the reading label, not the underlying evidence.

- **Required:** the topic supplies a prerequisite, critical control, main method/argument step or limitation needed to understand or assess the core claim. Explain the dependency in the opening and cover the necessary content here; do not send the reader away to obtain a missing premise.
- **Optional:** the core account already contains all necessary premises and evidence, and this topic adds procedural detail, additional derivation/validation or a nonessential extension. Say what extra understanding it adds and why the main reasoning remains followable if skipped.

Classify individual topics, not entire files: the same supplement can contain required evidence and optional procedural detail. Source location alone does not determine importance. An Extended Data experiment can be essential. If a required source is unavailable, record the specific gap and its effect; never relabel it optional to hide the gap. Reader-facing required/optional labels concern the value of reading that topic, not a demand to leave the explanation and open the source before proceeding.

Supporting scientific sources are distinct from personalized supplements based on old chats. A required supplemental control belongs in the core explanation. Optional personal connections still follow the complete relevant core topic and remain removable under the standalone-explanation contract.

## Save and review the path

Record small-topic titles, motivating problem/stakes, paper anchors, outcome/next question and supporting-source necessity/reason in the explanation's source crosswalk. Preserve the actual delivery boundary when splitting a chapter. Before delivery, check that no topic begins with an unexplained jump, that each reason identifies a real consequence, and that removing optional material leaves the core argument intact. Headings alone cannot fix a missing premise or an unsupported inference.
